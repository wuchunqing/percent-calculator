How Kegels work

Pelvic floor muscles support the bladder, uterus, and bowel. Repeated contractions build endurance and control — helping with leakage, recovery after childbirth, and core stability. This counter guides timed squeezes and rest periods so you train consistently.

Who benefits

  • Pregnancy and postpartum recovery
  • Stress or urge incontinence
  • Preparing for or after prostate surgery (men)
  • General pelvic floor maintenance

FAQ

How do I find the right muscles?

Try stopping urine flow mid-stream once to locate the muscles — do not make this a regular exercise. You should feel an inward lift, not thigh or buttock tension.

How often should I do Kegels?

Most guidelines suggest 3 sets of 10–15 repetitions, three times per day. Start with shorter holds and build up over 4–6 weeks.

When will I see results?

Some people notice improvement in 4–6 weeks with daily practice. Bladder control and strength continue to improve over 3 months.

Can Kegels cause harm?

Done correctly, they are safe for most people. Stop if you feel pain, increased pressure, or muscle fatigue. See a pelvic floor specialist if symptoms persist.
